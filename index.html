The king who tried to burn a warning

In the days of King Jehoiakim of Judah, the prophet Jeremiah dictated a scroll of his warnings to his scribe, Baruch ben Neriah. Baruch read it publicly, and eventually the scroll was brought before the king.

As it was read, Jehoiakim cut off each section with a scribe's knife and threw it into the fire burning beside him. The scroll was consumed piece by piece. Several officials urged the king not to destroy it, but he ignored them.

Then came the detail that gives the episode its force: Jeremiah was told to take another scroll. Baruch wrote the words again—and the biblical account says that many additional words were added. The king had destroyed the parchment. He had not destroyed the message.

The thought

There is a difference between silencing a warning and answering it. Jehoiakim treated an unwelcome message as though the problem were the document carrying it. The scroll went into the fire; the reality it described did not.

It is an uncomfortable question to carry into an ordinary day: when something tells me what I would rather not hear, do I respond to its truth—or try to get rid of the thing that brought it to me?

The date association is given by Chabad.org for 7 Kislev. The biblical narrative itself appears in Jeremiah 36. The exact dating of the episode is not independently established by the biblical chapter, so this page presents the traditional calendar association rather than claiming a modern historical date.
